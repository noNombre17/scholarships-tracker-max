# Max's Scholarship Journey

A plain-English walkthrough of how Max actually uses the app — no marketing, no fluff. Max is 17, lives on his phone, and has zero patience for forms. The app's whole job: tell him the *next* thing, make it one thumb, and get out of the way.

**The one rule the whole app is built on:** never show 57 things. Show 3–5, ranked by *money + how close the deadline is*. Everything else waits in the background until it matters.

---

## Flow 1 — First open (Aug 31, 2026)

**Trigger:** Max opens the app for the first time after his mom hands him the plan.

**Steps:**
1. No sign-up wall. It asks exactly one question: "What year are you?" → Max taps **Senior (class of 2027)**.
2. It pulls in what we already know: Rogers High School, U of A Fayetteville, CS + engineering + business, ACT 21, GPA 3.85, dad on active duty, mom at Walmart.
3. It gives him the honest headline in one line: **"You're chasing ~$250k across 57 scholarships. Here's your today."**
4. "Today" = exactly 3 cards, not a wall: **File FAFSA** (the blocker), **Coca-Cola Scholars** (Sep 30), **start your UA list**.
5. No dead-end buttons. Every card either opens a form or tells him why it matters.

**Done-state:** Max sees a clean "Next up" with 3 cards and knows his only job right now is tapping the first one. If he stops here, he still knows exactly what to do.

---

## Flow 2 — Daily / weekly 2-minute habit

**Trigger:** A phone notification ("Coca-Cola closes in 6 days") or Max opens the app because he's bored in the car.

**Steps:**
1. Opens straight to **Next Up**. The top card is the single most urgent thing — not a list of 57.
2. Taps it → sees the amount, the deadline in plain words ("Sep 30, 5pm ET — not midnight"), the effort ("phase 1: no essay, ~15 min"), and one big **Open / Start** button.
3. If it's a link-out (most no-essay items), one tap leaves the app and it remembers he started.
4. If it needs more than a tap, the app parks it in the monthly batch or the essay queue — it does **not** nag him again.

**Done-state:** The card flips to "started," his streak ticks up by one, and he's done in under 2 minutes. Never more than ~5 actionable cards on screen at once.

---

## Flow 3 — Monthly 10-minute re-entry batch (the 1st of the month)

**Trigger:** First of the month, or the first time he opens the app in a new month: **"Batch day — 8 re-entries, ~10 min."**

**Steps:**
1. One big card: "Monthly sweepstakes batch · 8 doors · ~10 min." A progress ring starts at 0/8.
2. Tap **Start batch.** The app walks a checklist one at a time: Sallie $2k, Niche $2k, SoFi $2.5k (only if 18+), Appily $1k, ScholarshipOwl $2,026, ScholarshipOwl $50k, Bold.org points, Scholly Easy Apply.
3. Each step = one line with a link-out. Tap **done** → checkmark, ring ticks up, next one slides in. His copy-paste answer bank is one tap away (see Flow 5).
4. He can pause and come back — nothing resets or double-counts.

**Done-state:** 8/8 → "Batch cleared — see you Oct 1." All the recurring items silently reset to "re-enter next month." Total time: ~10 minutes, one cup-of-coffee session.

---

## Flow 4 — Deadline crunch

**Trigger:** A hard deadline is close: **Coca-Cola Sep 30**, **UA admission Nov 1**, **UA scholarship Nov 15**.

**Steps:**
1. That ONE deadline gets promoted above everything — a big countdown chip, never buried in a list.
2. Tapping it shows a **crunch card:** what's due, exactly when, the effort, and what happens if he misses it (UA priority Nov 1 → final is Jan 15; scholarship priority Nov 15 → final is Feb 1).
3. If he's missing a required piece, the card calls it out: **"Blocker: FAFSA not filed yet"** or **"ACT retake Oct–Dec unlocks a higher UA tier."**
4. One button to do the thing, plus a "I'll do it this weekend" so it stops shouting but doesn't disappear.

**Done-state:** Submission logged → card drops into "Submitted / pending," and the next deadline automatically moves into the top slot. He never has to figure out what's next.

---

## Flow 5 — Essay reuse from the answer bank

**Trigger:** Max opens a scholarship that wants an essay, or taps an essay item in his queue.

**Steps:**
1. The app detects the essay type: leadership, "tell us about yourself," heritage, career goals, or "why U of A."
2. It offers the matching draft from his answer bank: **"Leadership (~250 words) — paste & trim."**
3. One tap copies it to the clipboard and shows the word count against the limit: **"250 words — you're at 240. Good."**
4. It nudges "trim before you submit" and saves what he actually used back with a tag — it never submits for him.

**Done-state:** The essay is saved as **"used for [scholarship]"** so the next reuse knows what's already floating around. Max copies, pastes, trims, submits — zero blank-page time.

---

## The rules Max never breaks

- He's 17 and hates forms → the app fills **zero** forms itself. It links out, copies, and remembers.
- Money talks first: amount + deadline, then effort, then fine print.
- One thumb, one tap, no dead ends.
- If it can wait, it waits. Only the next 3–5 things ever speak up.
